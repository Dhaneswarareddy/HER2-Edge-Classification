## Slide 2: Clinical Background and The HER2 Scoring Challenge

- **Slide Title**: Clinical Background: Why HER2 Scoring Demands Automation
- **Main Message**: Overexpression of HER2 dictates life-saving targeted therapy, but manual scoring under ASCO/CAP guidelines suffers from high inter-pathologist discordance.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Overexpressed in 15%–20% of invasive breast carcinomas; targets monoclonal antibody trastuzumab.
  - ASCO/CAP four-tier grading: 0 (Negative), 1+ (Negative), 2+ (Equivocal / Borderline), 3+ (Positive).
  - Differentiating 1+ from 2+ is notoriously subjective, with clinical disagreement exceeding 20%–30%.
  - Score 2+ requires expensive, time-consuming secondary confirmatory testing via FISH/DISH gene amplification.
- **Recommended Visual**: Diagram illustrating ASCO/CAP membrane staining criteria: faint incomplete membrane (1+) vs weak-moderate complete membrane (2+) vs intense circumferential membrane (3+).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "To understand the clinical stakes: HER2 is a transmembrane tyrosine kinase receptor. When the HER2 gene is amplified, cancer cells proliferate aggressively, but these tumours respond remarkably well to targeted therapies like Herceptin. Under standard ASCO/CAP clinical guidelines, pathologists score tissue sections into four categories: 0, 1+, 2+, and 3+. While 0 and 3+ are relatively straightforward, distinguishing 1+ from 2+ is a major clinical pain point. A misdiagnosis can either deny a patient life-saving targeted therapy or subject them to toxic and unnecessary treatments. Computer-aided digital pathology aims to bring objective, reproducible cell-level quantification to this diagnostic challenge."

## Slide 3: Motivation: From Synthetic cGANs (FYP2) to Real Clinical WSI

- **Slide Title**: The Research Gap: Does Synthetic Training Generalise to Real Biopsies?
- **Main Message**: Near-perfect performance on synthetic imagery gives a false sense of security; clinical histopathology introduces severe biological and optical heterogeneity.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - FYP2 Outcome: Deep CNNs trained on cGAN tiles achieved $>99.5\%$ accuracy and $1.000$ QWK.
  - Critical Hypothesis: Can cGAN-learned representations transfer directly to real-world clinical tissue?
  - Real-world obstacles: background stromal tissue, crush artifacts, red blood cells, batch stain shifts.
  - FYP3 Goal: Test this hypothesis on authentic clinical cohorts and measure the true domain gap.
- **Recommended Visual**: Side-by-side comparison of a pristine synthetic cGAN tile from FYP2 versus an authentic, heterogeneous BCI biopsy patch.
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "In FYP2, our models achieved over 99.5% accuracy on synthetic tiles generated by conditional GANs. But in the biomedical domain, models trained on synthetic data often fail completely when confronted with the chaos of clinical reality. Real Whole Slide Images contain tissue folds, varying slice thicknesses, lymphocytes, and non-tumour stroma. Our primary question at the start of FYP3 was: Do these synthetic representations actually hold up on authentic clinical biopsies? To answer this, we transitioned to the international Breast Cancer Immunohistochemical benchmark, BCI, comprising 4,873 clinical tiles across 51 whole slide image pairs."

## Slide 4: Data Provenance & Leakage Prevention Protocol

- **Slide Title**: Data Integrity: Preventing Spatial Leakage in Histopathology
- **Main Message**: Fragment-level grouping prevents optimistic data leakage that commonly plagues digital pathology benchmarks.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Tile level splitting causes severe data leakage through texture and background memorisation.
  - Reconstructed spatial slide fragments to isolate tissue structures into separate partitions.
  - BCI Grouped Partition: 3,388 Train (70%), 727 Validation (15%), 758 Test (15%). Zero fragment overlap.
  - External Cohort (HER2-IHC-40x): 107 acquisition blocks (86 development WSIs, 21 external test WSIs).
  - Crucial disclosure: BCI labels are slide-level diagnoses; agreement with third-party patch labels is 69.7%–73.6% on 2+.
- **Recommended Visual**: Architectural diagram showing spatial slide fragment clustering and clean partition separation without cross-split leakage.
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "In digital pathology, naive random splitting of patches is fatal: if tiles from the same patient or slide appear in both train and test sets, the neural network simply memorizes patient-specific background textures rather than learning cellular pathology. Because original slide metadata was omitted in the public BCI release, we algorithmically reconstructed slide fragment clusters from tile coordinate boundaries. All tiles from a given tissue fragment were locked exclusively into train, validation, or test. Furthermore, we must be completely candid about data provenance: BCI provides whole-slide diagnoses propagated to patches. Because breast tumours are biologically heterogeneous, slide-level labels only agree with micro-environmental patch labels approximately 70% of the time on borderline 2+ tissue."

## Slide 5: Phase A: In-Domain Real-Tissue Transfer on BCI

- **Slide Title**: Phase A Results: Real-Tissue Transfer across 9 Architectures
- **Main Message**: Real tissue reduces accuracy to 89%–94%; EfficientNetV2B0 emerges as the most efficient standard baseline.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Baseline accuracy drops by ~10 points compared to synthetic data (89.5%–90.8% validation accuracy).
  - EfficientNetV2B0 leads standard 224px models: Val QWK = 0.869, Test QWK = 0.915, Params = 5.9M.
  - DenseNet121 achieves strong test accuracy (94.2%), but requires 131 minutes to train.
  - Ordinal loss functions (CORN) failed to produce statistically significant improvements over cross-entropy.
  - Early stopping on validation QWK reliably prevents overfitting.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig1_bci_model_comparison.png` (Panel A: In-Domain Agreement across all architectures).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "As shown in Figure 1, the synthetic gap immediately manifested. On authentic BCI tissue, baseline validation accuracy across all nine deep architectures dropped to between 85% and 91%. Among standard 224-pixel models, EfficientNetV2B0 achieved the highest validation agreement, with a Quadratic Weighted Kappa of 0.869 and test QWK of 0.915, outperforming heavier networks like ResNet50 and ConvNeXtTiny while requiring only 5.9 million parameters. Interestingly, testing ordinal loss formulations through CORN provided no measurable advantage over standard cross-entropy. EfficientNetV2B0 was therefore established as our core backbone for in-depth optimization."

## Slide 6: E1 & E2: Spatial Resolution Optimization (The 448px Breakthrough)

- **Slide Title**: Resolution vs Batch Size: Doubling Input Size Unlocks Peak Performance
- **Main Message**: Spatial resolution is the single most decisive factor for HER2 classification ($\Delta\text{Val QWK} = +0.105^*$).
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Standard downsampling to 224px discards 95% of spatial pixels from a 1024px biopsy tile.
  - Experiment E1 (EffNetV2B0): $448\times448$ at batch 8 vs $224\times224$ at batch 8 and batch 32.
  - Paired Effect of Resolution: $\Delta\text{Val QWK} = +0.105$, $95\%\text{ CI } [0.056, 0.168]$ (Statistically Significant).
  - Paired Effect of Batch Size: $\Delta\text{Val QWK} = -0.040$, $95\%\text{ CI } [-0.109, +0.027]$ (Spans zero).
  - Establishes Primary Model: `EfficientNetV2B0__ce__hires` (Val QWK: 0.935, Test Acc: 0.975, Test QWK: 0.959).
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig2_paired_effects_forest.png` (Paired Effects Forest Plot).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "In Experiments E1 and E2, we investigated a critical physical question: When we downsample a 1024-pixel clinical patch to 224 pixels, we compress spatial area by a factor of 21. Does this blur away the cell membrane details necessary for scoring? We designed an experiment isolating resolution from mini-batch size. The results in Figure 2 are striking: doubling input resolution from 224 to 448 pixels delivered an enormous, statistically confirmed improvement of +10.5 points in validation QWK, reaching 0.935 validation QWK and 0.975 test accuracy. In contrast, varying the mini-batch size between 8 and 32 produced a confidence interval spanning zero. This proved that optical cell resolution, not training batch dynamics, is the primary driver of diagnostic accuracy."

## Slide 7: E4 & E5: Synthetic Pretraining & Domain Mixing Limits

- **Slide Title**: Domain Transfer: Can Synthetic cGAN Images Assist Real Training?
- **Main Message**: Synthetic cGAN pretraining and mixed augmentation offer no benefit over standard ImageNet features.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - E4 (`init_fyp2`): Initializing backbone with converged weights from FYP2 synthetic models.
  - E5 (`mix_fyp2`): Multi-domain training with 75% authentic BCI tiles and 25% synthetic FYP2 tiles.
  - E4 Paired Effect: $\Delta\text{Val QWK} = -0.015$, $95\%\text{ CI } [-0.075, +0.042]$ (Statistically indistinguishable from zero).
  - E5 Paired Effect: $\Delta\text{Val QWK} = -0.023$, $95\%\text{ CI } [-0.073, +0.020]$ (Statistically indistinguishable from zero).
  - Conclusion: Synthetic cGAN representations lack the micro-textures required to assist real-tissue feature learning.
- **Recommended Visual**: Forest plot subsection from `final_report_figures/fig2_paired_effects_forest.png` highlighting E4 and E5 confidence intervals crossing zero.
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "A core ambition of this project was to determine whether the synthetic images produced in FYP2 could serve as an effective pretraining prior. In Experiment E4, we initialized EfficientNetV2B0 with the synthetic weights, and in E5, we mixed 25% synthetic data into every training batch. As shown in the forest plot, both experiments resulted in negative point estimates on validation QWK (-0.015 and -0.023), with confidence intervals crossing zero. The scientific conclusion is definitive: while generative synthetic images are valuable for preliminary pipeline prototyping, they do not provide transferable diagnostic features that outperform standard natural image pretraining on ImageNet."

## Slide 8: E3: The Cross-Dataset Collapse on HER2-IHC-40x

- **Slide Title**: Cross-Dataset Evaluation: Catastrophic Zero-Shot Domain Collapse
- **Main Message**: Evaluating BCI-trained models on an independent external cohort results in complete failure due to 3+ collapse.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Tested on HER2-IHC-40x external test set: 1,847 tiles from 21 independent clinical WSIs.
  - Zero-shot accuracy collapsed to 41.9%–50.7%; balanced accuracy collapsed to near-chance (0.260–0.381).
  - Standard 224px EfficientNetV2B0: Accuracy = 0.448, Balanced Accuracy = 0.301, QWK = 0.108.
  - Diagnostic failure mechanism: 93% to 99% of all external tiles were predicted as Score 3+.
  - Even completely negative tumour sections (Score 0) were falsely classified as strongly positive.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig4_cross_dataset_collapse_and_harmonise.png` (Panel A: Raw Model Balanced Accuracy collapse).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "Next came the acid test of generalisation: Experiment E3. We took our BCI-trained models and evaluated them zero-shot on an external cohort of 1,847 tiles from an entirely independent clinical centre, HER2-IHC-40x. The models collapsed completely. Balanced accuracy plunged to near chance level—between 0.26 and 0.38. When we audited the confusion matrices, we discovered a catastrophic failure mode: the networks were predicting Score 3+ on over 93% of all external tiles! Even completely benign, unamplified Score 0 tissue was being misdiagnosed as metastatic 3+. Without addressing this domain shift, deep learning models cannot be safely deployed across different clinical acquisition cohorts."

## Slide 9: Optical Density Deconvolution & The Stain Shift Mechanism

- **Slide Title**: Diagnosing the Shift: Physical Stain Deconvolution Analysis
- **Main Message**: Inter-centre differences in brown DAB staining intensity and blue counterstaining cause systematic false-positive scoring.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Ruifrok-Johnston colour deconvolution maps RGB space into physical Optical Density (OD).
  - External cohort exhibits systematically elevated DAB optical density (median 0.48 vs 0.22 on BCI).
  - External cohort exhibits depressed Haematoxylin counterstaining.
  - CNNs learned that dark brown intensity corresponds to 3+ overexpression; target centre's darker baseline triggered false 3+ predictions.
  - Crucial insight: Feature ranking remains preserved ($AUC > 0.95$), but decision thresholds are severely shifted.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig3_stain_optical_density_distributions.png` (DAB and Haematoxylin OD profiles comparing BCI vs HER2-IHC-40x).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "To understand why this collapse occurred, we used Beer-Lambert optical density deconvolution to separate the brown DAB membrane stain from the blue Haematoxylin nuclear counterstain. Figure 3 reveals the root cause: the external centre's staining protocol produced a baseline DAB optical density more than double that of BCI. Because convolutional networks had learned that intense brown pigment indicates HER2 gene amplification, the entire external dataset was pushed over the decision threshold into Score 3+. Remarkably, the networks still ranked tiles correctly—achieving an AUC above 0.95—but their calibration thresholds were completely misaligned."

## Slide 10: HARMONISE-1: Unsupervised Stain Calibration Formulation

- **Slide Title**: HARMONISE-1: Unsupervised Stain Calibration without Retraining
- **Main Message**: Target-specific linear stain calibration restores +20.2 points of balanced accuracy on 224px models.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Objective: Align optical density distributions without retraining model weights or accessing test labels.
  - Linear gain transform in optical density space: $\text{OD}_H' = g_h \cdot \text{OD}_H$ and $\text{OD}_D' = g_d \cdot \text{OD}_D$.
  - Quantile matching on target development tiles derived frozen coefficients: $g_h = 0.2545, g_d = 0.5045$.
  - 224px EffNetV2B0 Results: Balanced accuracy restored from **0.301 to 0.503** ($\Delta = +0.202$).
  - Score 2+ Sensitivity: Recovered dramatically from **0.9% to 56.8%**.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig4_cross_dataset_collapse_and_harmonise.png` (Panels A & B showing recovery on 224px models).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "To solve this without needing hundreds of newly annotated training slides from every new clinical cohort, we designed HARMONISE: an unsupervised stain calibration pipeline. HARMONISE separates images into Haematoxylin and DAB optical density channels, applies empirical quantile-matching scale factors derived from unlabelled target development tiles, and reconstructs the RGB image before inference. As shown in Figure 4, on our standard 224-pixel EfficientNetV2B0 model, frozen stain calibration restored balanced accuracy from 0.301 to 0.503—a massive 20.2 percentage point recovery. Most importantly, sensitivity on borderline 2+ cases jumped from less than 1% to 56.8%."

## Slide 11: HARMONISE-2: Statistical Stability & The 448px Resolution Breakdown

- **Slide Title**: HARMONISE-2: Sampling Stability and The High-Resolution Paradox
- **Main Message**: Calibration requires $n \ge 1,000$ target tiles, and 448px high-resolution models fail under simple stain scaling.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Exp A: Bootstrap convergence proves $n \ge 1,000$ unlabelled tiles are required for parameter stability ($CV \le 0.05$).
  - Exp B: File-system audit confirmed that genuine slide identifiers are absent; spatial index runs are surrogates.
  - Exp D: 448px model collapsed under HARMONISE-1 (BalAcc dropped from 0.290 to 0.186; 3+ recall dropped to 0.081).
  - The Resolution Mechanism: At 448px, dimming Haematoxylin ($g_h = 0.2545$) destroys resolved nuclear context.
  - Scale compensation (`scale2`) recovered 448px balanced accuracy to 0.322, confirming optical coupling.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig6_harmonise2_bootstrap_and_flagging.png` (Panel A: Parameter CV vs sample size).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "In HARMONISE-2, we stress-tested this calibration mechanism. In Experiment A, bootstrap resampling proved that at least 1,000 unlabelled tiles from a new centre are required to guarantee statistical parameter stability. But we also encountered a critical scientific surprise in Experiment D: while stain calibration rescued the 224-pixel model, it broke the 448-pixel high-resolution model, cutting 3+ recall to just 8%. Why? At 448 pixels, the network resolves individual counterstained cell nuclei. Heavily dimming Haematoxylin stripped away the nuclear boundaries that define cell membranes. Applying multi-scale optical compensation restored balanced accuracy, demonstrating that stain calibration and spatial resolution are physically coupled."

## Slide 12: Class-Wise Failure Modes & The Clinical QWK Paradox

- **Slide Title**: Diagnostic Reality Check: The QWK Paradox and Borderline Weakness
- **Main Message**: High aggregate metrics can mask severe diagnostic blindness on clinically equivocal 1+ and 2+ categories.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - MobileNetV3Small achieved an apparent external Test QWK of **0.802** under HARMONISE-1.
  - Detailed per-class recall audit: Score 0 = 0.888, Score 3+ = 0.993, but **Score 1+ = 0.139 and Score 2+ = 0.090**; on EfficientNetV2B0 calibrated, Score 1+ recall is 0.177 and 2+ precision is 0.093.
  - The QWK Paradox: Polar separation of negative (0) and strongly positive (3+) inflates Quadratic Kappa.
  - Exp E (Confidence Triage): Flagging the top 40% most uncertain tiles discards **74%–79% of all true 2+ cases**.
  - Research Disclosure: Algorithmic assessment cannot yet safely replace human review on borderline HER2 tissue.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig5_classwise_performance_and_qwk_paradox.png` (Panel B: The QWK Paradox breakdown).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "Slide 12 presents the most important diagnostic finding of our project: The QWK Paradox. On paper, our calibrated MobileNet model achieved a spectacular Quadratic Weighted Kappa of 0.802 on external test data. But look closely at Panel B in Figure 5: while the model separated 0 from 3+ with near-perfection, it misclassified 86% of 1+ cases and 91% of 2+ cases. Because Quadratic Kappa heavily penalizes extreme errors while treating adjacent errors lightly, aggregate metrics can completely hide diagnostic failure on the exact borderline cases that clinicians care about most! Furthermore, when we attempted automated uncertainty flagging, the system preferentially discarded true 2+ cases. This unvarnished disclosure is vital: AI cannot yet autonomously adjudicate equivocal HER2 biopsies."

## Slide 14: Mobile Hardware Benchmarks: big.LITTLE Scaling, 46 FPS Streaming & Tri-Platform Comparison

- **Slide Title**: Mobile Hardware Benchmarks: Multi-Device Scaling (Samsung M35 & Realme 6) & SBC Comparison
- **Main Message**: Physical benchmarking across two heterogeneous smartphones reveals microarchitecture-dependent thread scaling (4T optimal on M35; 8T on Realme 6), sustained 26–46 FPS INT8 streaming, and decisive superiority over the Raspberry Pi 4B.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - **Samsung Galaxy M35 5G (Exynos 1380, 5nm)**:
    - 4-Thread Sweet Spot: 1T (214.2 ms) $\to$ 4T (**78.9 ms, 12.67 FPS**) yields 2.71× scaling; 8T degrades to 155.9 ms (+97.6% penalty) due to Cortex-A55 barrier sync stalls.
    - INT8 Fast Mode: **21.71 ms (46.06 FPS)** with 67% RAM reduction (**39.4 MB peak RSS**).
    - Thermal State: Flat at 28.8°C–29.1°C ($\Delta T = 0.3^\circ\text{C}$, `0x0` throttling) across >800 inferences.
  - **Realme 6 (MediaTek Helio G90T, 12nm)**:
    - 2T A76 Core Saturation: 1T (409.0 ms) $\to$ 2T (**228.8 ms**, 1.79× speedup).
    - 8T Aggregate Throughput: Reaches **145.19 ms (FP16)** and **38.63 ms (INT8, 25.89 FPS)** because 6× Cortex-A55 little cores overcome the barrier synchronization penalty.
    - Thermal State: Stable at 29.8°C–31.4°C ($\Delta T = 1.6^\circ\text{C}$, `0x0` throttling).
  - **Decisive Advantage over Raspberry Pi 4B**:
    - Primary FP16: Pi 4B = 353.5 ms (throttling at 84.2°C, `0x80008`) $\to$ Realme 6 = 145.2 ms (2.43× faster) $\to$ Samsung M35 = **78.9 ms (4.48× faster, 28.9°C)**.
    - INT8 Throughput: Samsung M35 is **16.28× faster** than Pi 4B (46.1 FPS vs 2.8 FPS), consuming 79% less RAM (39.4 MB vs 191.6 MB).
- **Recommended Visuals**: `final_report_figures/fig7_smartphone_edge_benchmark.png`, `fig9_mobile_app_interface_and_inference.png`, and `fig10_realme6_app_interface_and_inference.png`.
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "Slide 14 presents our physical edge hardware benchmark across two distinct mobile smartphones and the Raspberry Pi 4B. A fascinating microarchitectural contrast emerged: on the Samsung M35 with its 4 big A78 and 4 little A55 cores, 4 threads is the optimal sweet spot, delivering 78.9 milliseconds on FP16 and an extraordinary 21.7 milliseconds on INT8—over 46 frames per second! Dispatching 8 threads regressed latency to 155.9 ms because the fast cores stalled waiting for the slower efficiency cores. On the Realme 6 with 2 big A76 and 6 little A55 cores, 8 threads unlocked maximum burst throughput at 38.6 milliseconds on INT8 (26 FPS). Both smartphones completely outclassed the Raspberry Pi 4B, which required 353 milliseconds and overheated to 84.2°C, proving that modern smartphones are the true hardware frontier for point-of-care digital pathology."
