## Slide 2: Clinical Background and The HER2 Scoring Challenge

- **Slide Title**: Clinical Background: Why HER2 Scoring Needs Decision Support
- **Main Message**: HER2 status decides eligibility for anti-HER2 therapy, but manual scoring under the ASCO/CAP guideline is subjective, especially between 1+ and 2+.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - HER2 is over-expressed or amplified in about 15%–20% of invasive breast carcinomas; HER2 status decides eligibility for anti-HER2 therapy.
  - ASCO/CAP four-tier IHC score (2018 update; HER2-low addendum 2023): 0 and 1+ (HER2-negative; 1+ is HER2-low, relevant for trastuzumab deruxtecan), 2+ (equivocal), 3+ (positive).
  - Separating 1+ from 2+ is subjective: it is a continuum of membrane completeness and intensity.
  - Score 2+ requires reflex in situ hybridisation (ISH).
- **Recommended Visual**: Diagram illustrating ASCO/CAP membrane staining criteria: faint incomplete membrane (1+) vs weak-moderate complete membrane (2+) vs intense circumferential membrane (3+).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "To understand the clinical stakes: HER2 is over-expressed or amplified in about 15 to 20 percent of invasive breast cancers, and HER2 status decides whether a patient is eligible for anti-HER2 therapy. Under the ASCO/CAP guideline, pathologists score IHC sections as 0, 1+, 2+ or 3+. Since the 2023 HER2-low update, 1+ also matters, because HER2-low tumours can be treated with trastuzumab deruxtecan. 0 and 3+ are relatively clear; separating 1+ from 2+ is subjective, because membrane completeness and intensity form a continuum. A 2+ score triggers reflex in situ hybridisation. Automated scoring aims to make this assessment more reproducible, as support for, not a replacement of, the pathologist."

## Slide 3: Motivation: From Synthetic cGANs (FYP2) to Real IHC Tissue

- **Slide Title**: The Research Gap: Does Synthetic Training Generalise to Real Tissue?
- **Main Message**: Near-perfect performance on synthetic images says little about real histopathology, which adds biological and optical heterogeneity.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - FYP2 outcome: CNNs trained on cGAN tiles reached $>99.5\%$ accuracy on that synthetic distribution.
  - Critical hypothesis: Can cGAN-learned representations transfer to real tissue?
  - Real-world obstacles: artefacts, stroma, out-of-focus regions, batch staining variation, and different scanners and magnifications.
  - FYP3 goal: test this on real tissue from two laboratories and measure the domain gap.
- **Recommended Visual**: Side-by-side comparison of a synthetic cGAN tile from FYP2 versus a real BCI IHC tile.
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "In FYP2, our models exceeded 99.5% accuracy on synthetic tiles generated by conditional GANs. But GAN tiles lack real artefacts, stroma, out-of-focus regions and batch staining variation. Our first question in FYP3 was whether these synthetic representations hold up on real tissue. To answer it, we moved to the Breast Cancer Immunohistochemical dataset, BCI, published by Liu and colleagues at the CVPR Workshops in 2022, with 4,873 IHC tiles, and later to a second, external dataset from another laboratory."

## Slide 4: Data Provenance & Leakage Prevention Protocol

- **Slide Title**: Data Integrity: Preventing Spatial Leakage in Histopathology
- **Main Message**: Grouping tiles by reconstructed slide fragment prevents the leakage present in BCI's official split; label and provenance limits are disclosed.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - BCI releases no slide IDs, and its official split leaks: tiles cut side by side from one slide share a continuous border.
  - Slide fragments reconstructed by matching continuous tile edges: 2,763 accepted links, 691 fragments; whole fragments assigned to one split.
  - BCI grouped partition (70/15/15 recipe): 3,388 train, 727 validation, 758 test. No fragment in two splits.
  - External dataset HER2-IHC-40x (University of Malaya; *Data in Brief* 2025): 8,093-tile development archive and 1,847-tile WSI-based test split. The release reports 86/21 WSIs but gives no slide IDs, so tiles cannot be grouped by slide.
  - Disclosure: BCI labels are inherited from the parent WSI. Agreement with third-party refined patch labels is 82% overall, but only 69.7% (validation) and 73.6% (test) on 2+ tiles.
  - Disclosure: the external test split was examined during diagnostic audits, so every external result is exploratory, not a blind test.
- **Recommended Visual**: Diagram showing edge-matched slide fragments and their assignment to train, validation and test without cross-split overlap.
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "In digital pathology, random splitting of tiles leaks information: tiles cut side by side from one slide share a continuous border, so near-duplicate tissue appears in both training and test sets. BCI releases no slide IDs, and its official split has exactly this problem. We therefore linked tiles whose edges continue into each other, giving 691 slide fragments, and assigned whole fragments to train, validation or test. We must also be candid about the labels: BCI labels describe the whole slide the tile came from, not the tile itself. A third-party patch-level relabelling agrees with them on 82% of tiles overall, but only on about 70 to 74 percent of 2+ tiles. For the external dataset, the release reports 86 development and 21 test slides but gives no slide identifiers, and its test split was examined during our diagnostics, so external results are exploratory."

## Slide 5: Phase A: In-Domain Real-Tissue Transfer on BCI

- **Slide Title**: Phase A Results: Real-Tissue Transfer across 9 Architectures
- **Main Message**: Real tissue lowers validation accuracy to 85.4%–90.8%; EfficientNetV2B0 (CE) has the highest validation QWK among 224 px models.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Validation accuracy on real BCI tissue is 85.4%–90.8% across the baseline runs, against $>99.5\%$ on FYP2's GAN tiles.
  - EfficientNetV2B0 (CE) has the highest 224 px validation QWK: Val QWK = 0.869, Test QWK = 0.915, Params = 5.9M.
  - ResNet50 has the highest test accuracy (0.949) but a lower validation QWK (0.829). Test scores are higher than validation for every network: the test split is report-only and happens to be easier.
  - CORN gives no consistent gain: lower val QWK than CE for DenseNet121 (0.841 vs 0.854) and EfficientNetV2B0 (0.831 vs 0.869), higher for MobileNetV3Large (0.825 vs 0.800).
  - Early stopping on validation QWK (patience 10); three runs ended at the 45-epoch cap (ResNet50, MobileNetV3Large CORN, EfficientNetV2B0 `hires`). One training seed per configuration.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig1_bci_model_comparison.png` (Panel A: In-Domain Agreement across all architectures).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "As shown in Figure 1, the synthetic gap appeared immediately. On real BCI tissue, validation accuracy across the baseline networks fell to between 85.4 and 90.8 percent. Among standard 224-pixel models, EfficientNetV2B0 had the highest validation Quadratic Weighted Kappa, 0.869, with test QWK 0.915 and only 5.9 million parameters, so we selected it for the resolution study. Other networks, such as ResNet50, score higher on the test split, but test scores are higher than validation for every network, so we select on validation only. The ordinal CORN loss gave no consistent gain over cross-entropy."

## Slide 6: E1 & E2: Spatial Resolution (448 px vs 224 px)

- **Slide Title**: Resolution vs Batch Size: 448 px Input Improves EfficientNetV2B0
- **Main Message**: 448 px input is a confirmed improvement for EfficientNetV2B0 ($\Delta\text{Val QWK} = +0.105$, 95% CI [0.056, 0.168]); it was not confirmed for MobileNetV3Small.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Downsampling a 1024 px tile to 224 px reduces linear resolution 4.6×.
  - Design: 448 px at batch 8 (`hires`) vs a batch-matched 224 px control (`lowbatch`, batch 8) and the standard 224 px run (batch 32).
  - E1 resolution effect (EfficientNetV2B0): $\Delta\text{Val QWK} = +0.105$, $95\%\text{ CI } [0.056, 0.168]$; $\Delta\text{Test QWK} = +0.063$ $[0.026, 0.116]$. Both CIs exclude 0 (no p-values were computed).
  - E1 batch effect at 224 px: $\Delta\text{Val QWK} = -0.040$, $95\%\text{ CI } [-0.109, +0.027]$ (spans zero).
  - E2 (MobileNetV3Small): $\Delta\text{Val QWK} = +0.090$ $[-0.007, 0.207]$, $\Delta\text{Test QWK} = +0.017$ $[-0.034, 0.075]$: not confirmed.
  - Primary model: `EfficientNetV2B0__ce__hires` (Val QWK: 0.935, Test Acc: 0.975, Test QWK: 0.959).
  - Costs: 5.2× training time, higher device latency, and strong sensitivity to defocus (Slide 15). One seed per arm.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig2_paired_effects_forest.png` (Paired Effects Forest Plot).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "In Experiments E1 and E2 we asked a physical question: downsampling a 1024-pixel tile to 224 pixels reduces linear resolution 4.6 times. Does this blur away the membrane detail needed for scoring? We separated resolution from batch size by comparing 448 pixels at batch 8 with a 224-pixel control at the same batch size. For EfficientNetV2B0, 448 pixels improved validation QWK by 0.105, with a 95% confidence interval from 0.056 to 0.168, and test QWK by 0.063; both intervals exclude zero. The batch-size effect at 224 pixels was indistinguishable from zero. This gave our primary model, with validation QWK 0.935 and test accuracy 0.975. However, for MobileNetV3Small the resolution gain was not confirmed, so resolution is a confirmed improvement for one of two architectures, not a general rule. It also costs about five times the training time, higher latency on devices, and sensitivity to defocus."

## Slide 7: E4 & E5: Synthetic Pretraining & Domain Mixing Limits

- **Slide Title**: Domain Transfer: Can Synthetic cGAN Images Assist Real Training?
- **Main Message**: Synthetic cGAN pretraining and mixing gave no measurable change on BCI; on the colour-separable external set, synthetic-trained networks did best.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - E4 (`init_fyp2`): EfficientNetV2B0 initialised from the FYP2 synthetic model, then the same two-stage training on BCI.
  - E5 (`mix_fyp2`): ImageNet initialisation; FYP2's 1,680 synthetic training tiles added to the 3,388 BCI training tiles and shuffled together (≈33% synthetic on average, not a fixed ratio per batch).
  - E4 paired effect: $\Delta\text{Val QWK} = -0.015$, $95\%\text{ CI } [-0.075, +0.042]$ (CI spans zero).
  - E5 paired effect: $\Delta\text{Val QWK} = -0.023$, $95\%\text{ CI } [-0.073, +0.020]$ (CI spans zero).
  - Conclusion on BCI: FYP2's synthetic images did not help.
  - Caveat on HER2-IHC-40x: FYP2 synthetic-trained networks reach balanced accuracy 0.61–0.73 without calibration, better than every BCI network (224 px networks 0.41–0.53 with HARMONISE; the 448 px model 0.186). The external set was hue-filtered per class and is unusually separable by DAB colour (a 6-feature stain rule fitted on FYP2 reaches accuracy 0.89), so this reflects colour separability, not better tissue understanding.
- **Recommended Visual**: Forest plot subsection from `final_report_figures/fig2_paired_effects_forest.png` highlighting E4 and E5 confidence intervals crossing zero.
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "We also asked whether FYP2's synthetic images could serve as a pretraining prior. In E4 we initialised EfficientNetV2B0 from the synthetic model; in E5 we added FYP2's 1,680 synthetic training tiles to the BCI training set, about a third of the training pool. Both gave negative point estimates on validation QWK, minus 0.015 and minus 0.023, with confidence intervals crossing zero: no measurable change on BCI. There is one important twist. On the external dataset, the networks trained only on synthetic images actually scored better than any BCI network. The likely reason is that the external release selected tiles per class by colour, and FYP2's GAN classes also differ strongly in brown DAB colour. So the correct conclusion is narrow: synthetic training did not transfer to BCI, and colour-selected external benchmarks reward colour shortcuts."

## Slide 8: E3: The Cross-Dataset Collapse on HER2-IHC-40x

- **Slide Title**: Cross-Dataset Evaluation: Zero-Shot Collapse on HER2-IHC-40x
- **Main Message**: BCI-trained networks collapse on another laboratory's data, with widespread over-calling of 3+.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - External test split: 1,847 tiles (0/1+/2+/3+ = 658/316/111/762) from a different laboratory, scanner and magnification. Previously examined split; results are exploratory.
  - Ten BCI-trained networks: accuracy 0.419–0.586; balanced accuracy 0.260–0.381.
  - Standard 224 px EfficientNetV2B0: Accuracy = 0.448, Balanced Accuracy = 0.301, QWK = 0.108; 87.9% of tiles predicted 3+.
  - Failure mode: 43% to 94% of all external tiles are predicted 3+, including most true-0 tiles.
  - The 448 px model over-calls less (42.7% predicted 3+) but is no better overall (balanced accuracy 0.290).
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig4_cross_dataset_collapse_and_harmonise.png` (Panel A: Raw Model Balanced Accuracy collapse).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "Next came the test of generalisation, Experiment E3. We evaluated the BCI-trained networks, without retraining, on the 1,847-tile test split of HER2-IHC-40x, a dataset from the University of Malaya with a different laboratory, scanner and magnification. The networks collapsed: accuracy between 0.42 and 0.59, and balanced accuracy between 0.26 and 0.38, close to the 0.25 of chance. The confusion matrices show why: depending on the network, 43 to 94 percent of all external tiles were predicted 3+, including most true score-0 tiles. ## Slide 9: Optical Density Deconvolution & The Stain Shift Mechanism

- **Slide Title**: Diagnosing the Shift: Stain Deconvolution Analysis
- **Main Message**: The external set differs in both stains and in image scale: DAB is 2–3× higher in 1+, 2+ and 3+, haematoxylin is 3.4–7× stronger, and tiles are blurrier with larger stain blobs.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Ruifrok–Johnston colour deconvolution of optical density (OD), measured relative to each tile's own illumination, per class.
  - Median tile DAB (classes 0/1+/2+/3+): BCI 0.09/0.09/0.12/0.49 vs HER2-IHC-40x 0.05/0.16/0.31/1.29; lower for class 0, 2–3× higher for 1+, 2+ and 3+.
  - Haematoxylin is 3.4–7× stronger in HER2-IHC-40x (0.09–0.29 vs 0.02–0.08), not depressed.
  - External tiles are blurrier (sharpness 68–766 vs 554–2,279) and have larger detected stain blobs (5–14 px vs 2–3 px), consistent with a different magnification.
  - A network that learned "more brown = higher score" on BCI therefore over-calls on the external set.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig3_stain_optical_density_distributions.png` (DAB and Haematoxylin OD profiles comparing BCI vs HER2-IHC-40x).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "To understand the collapse, we used optical-density colour deconvolution to separate the brown DAB membrane stain from the blue haematoxylin counterstain, per class and relative to each tile's own illumination. Figure 3 shows that DAB is two to three times higher in the external 1+, 2+ and 3+ tiles than in BCI, while haematoxylin is 3.4 to 7 times stronger. The external tiles are also blurrier and their stain blobs are larger, consistent with a different magnification. A network that learned on BCI that more brown means a higher score will therefore over-call on the external data."

## Slide 10: HARMONISE-1: Unsupervised Stain Calibration Formulation

- **Slide Title**: HARMONISE-1: Unsupervised Stain Calibration without Retraining
- **Main Message**: Two optical-density gains raise 224 px EfficientNetV2B0 balanced accuracy from 0.301 to 0.503, but convert false 3+ calls into false 2+ calls.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Objective: align optical-density distributions without retraining or target labels. The reference domain is the BCI training tiles, so it is not reference-free, and it is an empirical calibration, not a physical stain normalisation.
  - Gain transform in optical-density space: $\text{OD}_H' = g_h \cdot \text{OD}_H$ and $\text{OD}_D' = g_d \cdot \text{OD}_D$, recombined as $I = I_0 \cdot 10^{-\text{OD}'}$.
  - Gains fitted by matching the 99th-percentile OD of 1,500 target development tiles to BCI training tiles: $g_h = 0.2545, g_d = 0.5045$.
  - 224 px EffNetV2B0 results: balanced accuracy **0.301 → 0.503** ($\Delta = +0.202$); QWK 0.108 → 0.593; 2+ recall 0.009 → 0.568, but 2+ precision only 0.093.
  - Clinical trade-off: negatives called 3+ fall from 758 to 64, but negatives called 2+ rise from 80 to 595 (61% of 974 negatives), and missed 2+ rise from 6 to 36.
  - The calibrated image must be passed to the network as float32: an 8-bit round trip changes 1.4% of predictions.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig4_cross_dataset_collapse_and_harmonise.png` (Panels A & B showing recovery on 224px models).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "To reduce the shift without new annotations, we designed HARMONISE, an unsupervised stain calibration. It separates each image into haematoxylin and DAB optical densities, scales them by two gains, and reconstructs the image before inference. The gains are fitted by matching the 99th-percentile optical density of unlabelled target development tiles to the BCI training tiles, so it needs that reference domain. On the 224-pixel EfficientNetV2B0, balanced accuracy rose from 0.301 to 0.503 and 2+ recall from under 1 percent to 57 percent. But the clinical picture is mixed: false 3+ calls among negatives fell from 758 to 64, while false 2+ calls rose from 80 to 595, that is 61 percent of negatives, and 2+ precision is only 0.093. In other words, calibration moved the errors rather than removing them."

## Slide 11: HARMONISE-2: Statistical Stability & The 448px Failure

- **Slide Title**: HARMONISE-2: Sampling Stability and the 448 px Failure
- **Main Message**: Stable gains need about 1,000 unlabelled target tiles, and the 448 px model breaks under calibration; the development-tile dose-response points to the haematoxylin gain.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Exp A (1,000 bootstrap resamples): CV(g_d) = 0.142 at n = 100; at n = 1,000, CV(g_h) = 0.033 and CV(g_d) = 0.055. At least about 1,000 unlabelled tiles are needed for CV ≈ 0.05.
  - Exp B: neither dataset contains slide metadata; filename runs are acquisition sequences, so slide- or patient-level grouping is impossible.
  - 448 px model on the external test split under HARMONISE-1: balanced accuracy 0.290 → 0.186, QWK −0.155, 3+ recall 0.802 → 0.081.
  - Exp D dose-response (1,200 development tiles, 3+ recall): no calibration 0.849; DAB gain only 0.852; **haematoxylin gain only 0.266**; both gains 0.307. The collapse follows the haematoxylin gain; why haematoxylin matters more at 448 px was not tested.
  - Scale compensation (`frozen_scale2`), same development tiles: balanced accuracy 0.279 (none) → 0.228 (frozen) → 0.322 (+0.043), but it **failed** the pre-set acceptance rule (3+ recall fell by more than 0.10). It cannot be compared with the 0.186 test result (different data sets).
  - AdaBN, by contrast, rescues the 448 px model on the test split (0.186 → 0.516; Slide 15).
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig6_harmonise2_bootstrap_and_flagging.png` (Panel A: Parameter CV vs sample size).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "In HARMONISE-2 we stress-tested the calibration on unused development tiles. Bootstrap resampling showed that about 1,000 unlabelled tiles from a new site are needed for the gains to be stable. We also confirmed that neither dataset has slide metadata. The main surprise was the 448-pixel model: calibration broke it, cutting 3+ recall on the test split from 0.80 to 0.08. A dose-response test on development tiles separates the two gains: dimming DAB alone leaves 3+ recall unchanged at 0.85, while dimming haematoxylin alone drops it to 0.27. So the failure follows the haematoxylin gain; why the high-resolution model depends on it more, we did not test. A scale-compensated variant improved development balanced accuracy only slightly and failed our pre-set acceptance rule. AdaBN, which we show later, does rescue this model."

## Slide 12: Class-Wise Failure Modes & The Clinical QWK Paradox

- **Slide Title**: Diagnostic Reality Check: The QWK Paradox and Borderline Weakness
- **Main Message**: High aggregate metrics can mask failure on the clinically equivocal 1+ and 2+ categories.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - MobileNetV3Small reaches an external test QWK of **0.802** under HARMONISE-1.
  - Per-class recall: Score 0 = 0.888, Score 3+ = 0.993, but **Score 1+ = 0.139 and Score 2+ = 0.090**; 47% of true 1+ tiles are called 3+. EfficientNetV2B0 calibrated: 1+ recall 0.177, 2+ precision 0.093. DenseNet121: 1+ and 2+ recall 0.000.
  - The QWK paradox: quadratic weights penalise large ordinal errors, so separating 0 from 3+ dominates the score.
  - Exp E (confidence referral, development tiles): at target coverage 0.5 (realised 0.44–0.46), EfficientNetV2B0 + HARMONISE refers **74%–79% of true 2+ tiles**; across networks, 76%–94%. Out of domain, referral concentrates on 2+ and cannot replace review of equivocal cases.
  - Research disclosure: tile-level automated scoring cannot replace pathologist review of borderline HER2 tissue.
- **Recommended Visual**: `final_report_figures/fig5_classwise_performance_and_qwk_paradox.png` (Panel B: The QWK Paradox breakdown).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "Slide 12 shows what we call the QWK paradox. Our calibrated MobileNet reached a Quadratic Weighted Kappa of 0.802 on the external test split, which looks strong. But Figure 5 shows that while it separated 0 from 3+ well, it misclassified 86 percent of 1+ tiles and 91 percent of 2+ tiles, and called almost half of the true 1+ tiles 3+. Because the quadratic weights penalise large ordinal errors heavily and adjacent errors lightly, the aggregate metric hides failure on exactly the borderline cases clinicians care about. When we tried confidence-based referral on development tiles, at about 50 percent coverage the system referred 74 to 79 percent of true 2+ tiles for the calibrated EfficientNet. Per-class recall and error-type counts should therefore be mandatory, and equivocal cases still need a pathologist."

## Slide 14: Edge Benchmarks: Samsung M35, Realme 6 and Raspberry Pi 4B (measured 10 Nov 2026)

- **Slide Title**: Edge Benchmarks: Two Phones and a Raspberry Pi 4B under One Protocol
- **Main Message**: The deployed 448 px FP16 model needs a median 81.4 ms per tile on the Samsung (4 threads), 139.1 ms on the Realme (8 threads) and 336.5 ms on the Pi (4 threads); INT8 is 3–4× faster but rejected for accuracy.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - **Protocol**: one protocol on all devices; 10 warm-up runs, then 100 timed invokes; 60 s cool-down between phone configurations; a fresh process per Pi configuration; no CPU affinity set anywhere. Latency is network time per tile (decode and resize excluded).
  - **Samsung Galaxy M35 5G** (448 px FP16): 214.9 ms (1 thread) → 119.0 ms (2) → **81.4 ms (4; p95 107.5)**. 8 threads is slower and much more variable (95.1 ms, p95 729.4). Peak memory 120 MB.
  - **Realme 6**: 301.7 ms (1 thread) → 174.9 → 142.6 → **139.1 ms (8; p95 181.8)**. Peak memory 124 MB.

## Slide 15: Extended Evaluation (`experiments_v2`)

- **Slide Title**: Extended Evaluation: Clinical Cost, Uncertainty, Explanation, Robustness and Adaptation
- **Main Message**: In-domain, the 448 px model has the lowest clinical cost and supports referral but is sensitive to defocus; across laboratories, a few labelled tiles beat every unsupervised fix.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - **Clinical cost** (illustrative workflow weights, ±50% sensitivity): BCI test 448 px 0.12 [0.06, 0.22] vs 224 px 0.31 [0.20, 0.48], paired Δ −0.19 [−0.33, −0.09]; 448 px cheapest in 100% of cost draws (sensitivity analysis on BCI validation, all 20 runs). Externally, the FYP2 ResNet50 (raw) has the lowest cost (0.54).
  - **Uncertainty**: BCI test ECE 0.010–0.078. 448 px model at 93.7% coverage: accuracy 0.975 → 0.989, catching 58% of errors while referring 3.7% of 2+ tiles. Externally, 50.5% of 2+ tiles are referred at 70% coverage.
  - **Grad-CAM** (7,057 tiles; 7×7/14×14 maps cannot resolve membranes): correct 3+ calls attend to strongly brown regions (DAB ratio 1.23–1.29 on BCI, 1.22–1.42 externally); correct external 1+ calls attend to glass and pale areas (background ratio 1.07–1.23), a likely shortcut.
  - **Robustness**: brightness ±15% costs 12–19 points of balanced accuracy; the 448 px model is defocus-sensitive (blur σ = 0.9 µm costs 0.17 vs 0.03 at 224 px; σ = 1.8 µm costs 0.42). JPEG ≥ 70, sensor noise, ±20% DAB and haematoxylin ×1.2 have negligible effect; haematoxylin ×0.8 costs about 3 points.
  - **AdaBN** (5 networks): +0.06 to +0.23 test balanced accuracy over raw input; against HARMONISE it is network-dependent (−0.13 MobileNetV3Small, tie for 224 px EfficientNetV2B0, +0.33 for the 448 px model). Best unsupervised external balanced accuracy ≈ 0.52–0.53.
  - **Few-shot** (224 px EfficientNetV2B0, full fine-tuning, 5 support draws): k = 1 per class 0.544 ± 0.024; **k = 5: 0.782 ± 0.053** (QWK 0.918); k = 20: 0.845 ± 0.048. Zero-shot 0.301, HARMONISE 0.503, AdaBN 0.522.
  - **Ablation**: frozen ImageNet backbone with a linear head costs 0.193 val accuracy and 0.267 val QWK; an MLP head does not help; unfreezing only `block6` + `top` costs 0.089 val QWK.
- **Recommended Visual**: `experiments_v2/06_fewshot/outputs/fig_fewshot_data_efficiency.png` and `experiments_v2/04_robustness/outputs/fig_robustness.png` (optionally `experiments_v2/01_clinical_risk/outputs/fig_error_taxonomy.png`).
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "After the main study we ran a set of extended experiments, with every choice fixed on validation or development data. Weighting errors by their clinical consequence, the 448-pixel model has the lowest expected cost on BCI under every cost weighting tested, and its confidence supports a sensible referral workflow: referring about 6 percent of tiles raises accuracy from 0.975 to 0.989. Its weakness is image quality: mild defocus costs it much more than the 224-pixel model, which matters for camera-based use. Across laboratories, Grad-CAM suggests the external 1+ calls rely on glass and pale areas. Unsupervised fixes reach about 0.5 balanced accuracy at best, while fine-tuning on just 5 labelled tiles per class reaches 0.78. The practical route to a new site is therefore a small labelled set, fine-tuning, and pathologist review of 2+ tiles."

## Slide 16: Conclusions, Scientific Disclosures, and Future Outlook

- **Slide Title**: Conclusions: Summary of Contributions & Future Work
- **Main Message**: A leakage-controlled BTech study connecting histopathology, deep learning, domain adaptation and edge deployment, with its limits stated.
- **Key Contributions**:
  1. Real-Tissue Benchmark: Reconstructed slide fragments by edge matching and established EfficientNetV2B0 448 px as the primary model (Val QWK: 0.935, Test Acc: 0.975, Test QWK: 0.959). The resolution gain is confirmed for EfficientNetV2B0, not for MobileNetV3Small.
  2. Synthetic Evaluation: FYP2 synthetic pretraining or mixing gave no measurable change on BCI; synthetic-trained networks did best on the colour-separable external set.
  3. Cross-Laboratory Adaptation: HARMONISE raises 224 px EfficientNetV2B0 balanced accuracy by +0.202 (0.301 → 0.503) but shifts errors to 2+ and breaks the 448 px model; AdaBN reaches about 0.52; 5 labelled tiles per class reach 0.782.
  4. Diagnostic Integrity: Exposed the QWK paradox and added a clinical error taxonomy; 1+/2+ separation remains unreliable.
  5. Mobile & Edge Deployment: Android app v1.1 reproduces the validated predictions on 758/758 BCI test tiles on both phones (accuracy 0.975); median 448 px latency 81.4 ms (Samsung), 139.1 ms (Realme), 336.5 ms (Pi 4B). INT8 rejected; no power measured.
- **Disclosures**: research software, not clinically validated; tile level only, no slide IDs; one training seed per configuration; the external test split was examined before, so external results are exploratory.
- **Future Directions**: Slide-level aggregation with slide-identified data and pathologist review of errors and Grad-CAM maps; multi-seed training and a held-out third laboratory for unbiased external testing; small labelled sets per site with an acceptance protocol; a focus and brightness quality check before inference and power measurement on devices; hardware acceleration (Android NNAPI/GPU delegates, Coral/Hailo) with accuracy gates.
- **Recommended Visual**: High-level summary flowchart depicting the pipeline from IHC tile to on-device prediction.
- **Speaker Script**:
  > "To conclude: FYP3 moved from synthetic GAN benchmarks to real IHC tissue with a leakage-controlled split. Higher input resolution was a confirmed improvement for EfficientNetV2B0, giving test accuracy 0.975, though not for MobileNetV3Small. Synthetic pretraining did not help on BCI. Across laboratories, the networks collapsed; unsupervised calibration and AdaBN recovered about half of the gap, with different clinical error profiles, while a few labelled target tiles recovered much more. We documented the weakness of automated scoring on equivocal 1+ and 2+ cases, and we deployed the model in an Android app whose on-device predictions match the validated model on all 758 test tiles on two phones. This is research software, not a medical device. We thank the committee for their time and welcome your questions."
